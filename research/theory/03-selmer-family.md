# A complete descent by 2-isogeny for $y^2 = x^3 + p^2x$, $p \equiv 5 \pmod 8$

**Wave 3 — original theory.** This note performs a full descent by 2-isogeny on a positive-density
family of quartic twists of $j = 1728$ curves, computes both isogeny Selmer groups exactly, and
deduces the Mordell–Weil group and the triviality of $\Sha[2]$. The family is disjoint from the
Wave-2 family $y^2 = x^3 - p^2x$, $p \equiv 3 \pmod 8$ (see the Honesty section for the proof of
disjointness).

---

## 1. Statement

For a prime $p$ let

$$E_p:\; y^2 = x^3 + p^2 x, \qquad E'_p:\; Y^2 = X^3 - 4p^2 X .$$

Both curves carry the rational 2-torsion point $T = (0,0)$, resp. $T' = (0,0)$, and there is the
classical 2-isogeny

$$\varphi: E_p \to E'_p, \qquad \varphi(x,y) = \Big( \frac{y^2}{x^2},\; \frac{y(x^2 - p^2)}{x^2} \Big),
\qquad \ker\varphi = \{O, T\},$$

with dual $\hat\varphi : E'_p \to E_p$ satisfying $\hat\varphi \circ \varphi = [2]_{E_p}$ and
$\varphi \circ \hat\varphi = [2]_{E'_p}$ (Silverman, *AEC*, III.4.5 and X.4.9).

> **Theorem.** Let $p \equiv 5 \pmod 8$ be prime (a set of primes of Dirichlet density $1/4$). Then,
> inside $\mathbb{Q}^\times/(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$,
>
> $$\operatorname{Sel}^{(\hat\varphi)}(E'_p/\mathbb{Q}) = \{1\}, \qquad
> \operatorname{Sel}^{(\varphi)}(E_p/\mathbb{Q}) = \{1,\,-1,\,2p,\,-2p\} \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)^2,$$
>
> and the second group is generated by the images of the rational $2$-torsion points of $E'_p$.
> Consequently
>
> $$\operatorname{rank} E_p(\mathbb{Q}) = 0, \qquad
> E_p(\mathbb{Q}) = \{O, (0,0)\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \qquad
> E'_p(\mathbb{Q}) = \{O', (0,0), (\pm 2p, 0)\} \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)^2,$$
>
> and moreover $\Sha(E_p/\mathbb{Q})[2] = \Sha(E'_p/\mathbb{Q})[2] = 0$, hence both $2$-primary
> parts $\Sha(E_p/\mathbb{Q})[2^\infty]$, $\Sha(E'_p/\mathbb{Q})[2^\infty]$ vanish.

Every nontrivial candidate Selmer class is killed at a **named place**: the archimedean place, the
place $2$ (valuation parity), or the place $p$ (a quadratic- or quartic-power obstruction in
$\mathbb{F}_p^\times$). The congruence $p \equiv 5 \pmod 8$ is used only through Lemma A, in
Sections 4 and 5, and is sharp for this method (Section 8).

## 2. The descent apparatus

We use descent by 2-isogeny in the form of Silverman, *AEC* (2nd ed.), Prop. X.4.9, specialized to
$a = 0$. Write $b = p^2$, so $E = E_p: y^2 = x^3 + bx$ and $E' = E'_p: Y^2 = X^3 - 4bX$.

**Descent maps.** Define homomorphisms

$$\alpha: E(\mathbb{Q}) \to \mathbb{Q}^\times/(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2, \qquad
\alpha(O) = 1,\quad \alpha(T) = b \equiv 1,\quad \alpha(x,y) = x \ \ (x \neq 0);$$

$$\alpha': E'(\mathbb{Q}) \to \mathbb{Q}^\times/(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2, \qquad
\alpha'(O') = 1,\quad \alpha'(T') = -4b \equiv -1,\quad \alpha'(X,Y) = X \ \ (X \neq 0).$$

By *loc. cit.*, $\alpha$ and $\alpha'$ are group homomorphisms with

$$\ker \alpha = \hat\varphi\big(E'(\mathbb{Q})\big), \qquad \ker \alpha' = \varphi\big(E(\mathbb{Q})\big),$$

so $\operatorname{im}\alpha \cong E(\mathbb{Q})/\hat\varphi(E'(\mathbb{Q}))$ and
$\operatorname{im}\alpha' \cong E'(\mathbb{Q})/\varphi(E(\mathbb{Q}))$.

**Homogeneous spaces.** For a squarefree integer $d \neq 0$ define the quartic curves

$$C_d:\; w^2 = d\,u^4 + \frac{p^2}{d}\,v^4, \qquad\qquad
C'_d:\; w^2 = d\,u^4 - \frac{4p^2}{d}\,v^4,$$

in the variables $(u,v,w)$ with $(u,v) \neq (0,0)$, weighted so that
$(u,v,w) \mapsto (\lambda u, \lambda v, \lambda^2 w)$ acts on solutions. Over any field
$K \supseteq \mathbb{Q}$ (or $\mathbb{Q}_v$), a point of $C_d(K)$ with $uv \neq 0$ gives the point

$$\Big( d\,\frac{u^2}{v^2},\; d\,\frac{uw}{v^3} \Big) \in E(K),
\qquad \text{since} \qquad
\frac{d^2u^2w^2}{v^6} = \frac{d^3u^6}{v^6} + b\,\frac{du^2}{v^2},$$

whose $x$-coordinate lies in the class $d$; points with $v = 0$ (forcing $d \equiv 1$) correspond to
$O$, and points with $u = 0$ (forcing $d \equiv b$) to $T$. The same formulas with $-4b$ in place of
$b$ relate $C'_d$ to $E'$. Conversely (Silverman X.4.9(c)), for squarefree $d$:

$$d \in \operatorname{im}\alpha \iff C_d(\mathbb{Q}) \neq \emptyset, \qquad
d \in \operatorname{im}\alpha' \iff C'_d(\mathbb{Q}) \neq \emptyset,$$

and the identical statement holds over each completion $\mathbb{Q}_v$ for the local Kummer images.
Identifying $H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E[\varphi]) \cong H^1(\mathbb{Q}, \mu_2) \cong
\mathbb{Q}^\times/(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$ (the Galois action on $E[\varphi] = \{O,T\}$ is trivial),
the Selmer groups become

$$\operatorname{Sel}^{(\varphi)}(E/\mathbb{Q}) = \{\, d \text{ squarefree} : C'_d(\mathbb{Q}_v) \neq \emptyset \ \forall v \,\},
\qquad
\operatorname{Sel}^{(\hat\varphi)}(E'/\mathbb{Q}) = \{\, d : C_d(\mathbb{Q}_v) \neq \emptyset \ \forall v \,\},$$

and there are exact sequences (Silverman X.4.2, applied to $\varphi$ and $\hat\varphi$)

$$0 \to E'(\mathbb{Q})/\varphi(E(\mathbb{Q})) \xrightarrow{\ \alpha'\ } \operatorname{Sel}^{(\varphi)}(E/\mathbb{Q}) \to \Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi] \to 0,$$
$$0 \to E(\mathbb{Q})/\hat\varphi(E'(\mathbb{Q})) \xrightarrow{\ \alpha\ } \operatorname{Sel}^{(\hat\varphi)}(E'/\mathbb{Q}) \to \Sha(E'/\mathbb{Q})[\hat\varphi] \to 0,$$

where $\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi] := \ker\big(\varphi_*: \Sha(E/\mathbb{Q}) \to \Sha(E'/\mathbb{Q})\big)$
and dually.

**Step 0 (support of candidate classes).** *If $d$ is squarefree and $q \nmid 2p$ is a prime with
$q \mid d$, then $C_d(\mathbb{Q}_q) = C'_d(\mathbb{Q}_q) = \emptyset$. Hence every Selmer class on
either side lies in*

$$\Delta := \{\pm 1,\, \pm 2,\, \pm p,\, \pm 2p\} \subset \mathbb{Q}^\times/(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2 .$$

*Proof.* Multiply $C_d$ by $d^2$ and set $W = dw$: the equation becomes
$W^2 = d\,(d^2u^4 + p^2 v^4)$ with integral coefficients. Normalize a putative solution so that
$u, v \in \mathbb{Z}_q$ with $\min(v_q(u), v_q(v)) = 0$; then $W^2 \in \mathbb{Z}_q$. Since
$q \mid d$, $q \nmid p$, $d$ squarefree:

* if $q \nmid v$: $v_q(d^2u^4) \geq 2$ and $v_q(p^2v^4) = 0$, so $v_q(W^2) = v_q(d) = 1$, odd — impossible;
* if $q \mid v$ (so $q \nmid u$): $v_q(d^2u^4) = 2$ and $v_q(p^2v^4) \geq 4$, so $v_q(W^2) = 1 + 2 = 3$, odd — impossible.

The same computation applies verbatim to $W^2 = d(d^2u^4 - 4p^2v^4)$, using $q \neq 2$. $\square$

**Rank identity.** *With $|E(\mathbb{Q})[2]| = 2$ (proved in Section 6 below),*

$$2^{\,\operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q})+1} \;=\; \tfrac12\,\lvert \operatorname{im}\alpha\rvert \cdot \lvert\operatorname{im}\alpha'\rvert .$$

*Proof.* Since $2E(\mathbb{Q}) = \hat\varphi(\varphi(E(\mathbb{Q})))$,

$$|E(\mathbb{Q})/2E(\mathbb{Q})| = [E(\mathbb{Q}) : \hat\varphi E'(\mathbb{Q})]\cdot[\hat\varphi E'(\mathbb{Q}) : 2E(\mathbb{Q})]
= |\operatorname{im}\alpha| \cdot [\hat\varphi E'(\mathbb{Q}) : 2E(\mathbb{Q})].$$

The surjection $\psi: E'(\mathbb{Q}) \twoheadrightarrow \hat\varphi E'(\mathbb{Q})/2E(\mathbb{Q})$,
$\psi(P') = \hat\varphi(P') \bmod 2E(\mathbb{Q})$, has kernel
$\varphi(E(\mathbb{Q})) + \ker\hat\varphi(\mathbb{Q})$ with $\ker\hat\varphi(\mathbb{Q}) = \{O', T'\}$.
Now $T' \notin \varphi(E(\mathbb{Q}))$: from the formula for $\varphi$, a point of $E$ maps to
$T' = (0,0)$ iff its $y$-coordinate vanishes and its $x$-coordinate is nonzero, i.e. iff it is
$(\pm ip, 0)$ with $i^2 = -1$ — not rational. So $\ker\psi = \varphi E(\mathbb{Q}) \sqcup
(T' + \varphi E(\mathbb{Q}))$ has index $|\operatorname{im}\alpha'|/2$ in $E'(\mathbb{Q})$, giving
$|E(\mathbb{Q})/2E(\mathbb{Q})| = \tfrac12 |\operatorname{im}\alpha|\,|\operatorname{im}\alpha'|$.
Finally $E(\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^r \oplus E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}$ gives
$|E(\mathbb{Q})/2E(\mathbb{Q})| = 2^r\,|E(\mathbb{Q})[2]| = 2^{r+1}$. $\square$

## 3. Character lemma at $p$

**Lemma A.** Let $p \equiv 5 \pmod 8$. Then, in $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$:

1. $\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right) = +1$, $\left(\frac{2}{p}\right) = -1$, $\left(\frac{-2}{p}\right) = -1$;
2. $-1$ is **not** a fourth power;
3. $4$ is **not** a fourth power.

*Proof.* (1) $(-1/p) = (-1)^{(p-1)/2} = +1$ since $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$; with $p = 8k+5$,
$(p^2-1)/8 = (2k+1)(4k+3)$ is odd, so $(2/p) = (-1)^{(p^2-1)/8} = -1$; multiply for $(-2/p)$.
(2) If $z^4 = -1$ in $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ then $z$ has multiplicative order $8$, so
$8 \mid p - 1$, contradicting $p \equiv 5 \pmod 8$.
(3) If $z^4 = 4$ then $(z^2-2)(z^2+2) = 0$, so $2$ or $-2$ would be a square, contradicting (1). $\square$

## 4. The $\hat\varphi$-Selmer group: $\operatorname{Sel}^{(\hat\varphi)}(E'_p/\mathbb{Q}) = \{1\}$

By Step 0 the candidates are $d \in \Delta = \{\pm 1, \pm 2, \pm p, \pm 2p\}$, with spaces
$C_d: w^2 = du^4 + (p^2/d)v^4$. The class $1$ lies in the Selmer group ($C_1$ has the point
$(1,0,1)$, the image of $O$; note also $\alpha(T) = p^2 \equiv 1$). We kill the seven others, each
at one named place.

**At $\infty$ ($d = -1, -2, -p, -2p$).** Both coefficients $d$ and $p^2/d$ are negative, so for
$(u,v) \neq (0,0)$ real, $du^4 + (p^2/d)v^4 < 0 \le w^2$. Hence $C_d(\mathbb{R}) = \emptyset$.

**At $2$ ($d = 2$ and $d = 2p$).** Clearing denominators ($W = 2w$):

$$C_2:\; W^2 = 2\,(4u^4 + p^2v^4), \qquad C_{2p}:\; W^2 = 2p\,(4u^4 + v^4).$$

Normalize $u, v \in \mathbb{Z}_2$ not both even. If $v$ is odd, the inner factor
$4u^4 + p^2v^4$ (resp. $4u^4 + v^4$) is odd, so $v_2(W^2) = 1$ — odd, impossible. If $v = 2v_1$
(so $u$ is odd), the inner factor equals $4(u^4 + 4p^2v_1^4)$ (resp. $4(u^4 + 4v_1^4)$) with odd
second factor, so $v_2(W^2) = 3$ — again odd, impossible. Hence
$C_2(\mathbb{Q}_2) = C_{2p}(\mathbb{Q}_2) = \emptyset$ (for **every** odd $p$; no congruence needed).

**At $p$ ($d = p$).** $C_p: w^2 = p\,(u^4 + v^4)$. Normalize $u, v \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ not both in
$p\mathbb{Z}_p$. If $p$ divides exactly one of $u, v$, the factor $u^4 + v^4$ is a unit and
$v_p(w^2) = 1$ — impossible; $p$ cannot divide both. So $u, v \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ and
$v_p(w^2)$ even forces $u^4 + v^4 \equiv 0 \pmod p$, i.e. $(u/v)^4 \equiv -1$ in
$\mathbb{F}_p^\times$. By Lemma A(2) this is impossible. Hence $C_p(\mathbb{Q}_p) = \emptyset$.

Therefore $\operatorname{Sel}^{(\hat\varphi)}(E'_p/\mathbb{Q}) = \{1\}$, and since
$\operatorname{im}\alpha \subseteq \operatorname{Sel}^{(\hat\varphi)}$,

$$\operatorname{im}\alpha = \{1\}, \qquad \Sha(E'_p/\mathbb{Q})[\hat\varphi] = 0 . \tag{4.1}$$

## 5. The $\varphi$-Selmer group: $\operatorname{Sel}^{(\varphi)}(E_p/\mathbb{Q}) = \{\pm 1, \pm 2p\}$

Candidates again $d \in \Delta$, with spaces $C'_d: w^2 = du^4 - (4p^2/d)v^4$.

**Four classes come from global $2$-torsion.** $E'_p$ has full rational 2-torsion,
$X^3 - 4p^2X = X(X-2p)(X+2p)$, and

$$\alpha'(O') = 1, \quad \alpha'(T') = -4p^2 \equiv -1, \quad \alpha'\big((2p, 0)\big) = 2p, \quad \alpha'\big((-2p, 0)\big) = -2p .$$

These four classes are pairwise distinct in $\mathbb{Q}^\times/(\mathbb{Q}^\times)^2$: every
pairwise ratio lies in $\{-1,\, 2p,\, -2p\}$, and none of these is a rational square since $p$ is an
odd prime. Hence

$$\{1, -1, 2p, -2p\} \subseteq \operatorname{im}\alpha' \subseteq \operatorname{Sel}^{(\varphi)}(E_p/\mathbb{Q}).$$

Concretely, $C'_{2p}: w^2 = 2p(u^4 - v^4)$ contains $(1,1,0)$, $C'_{-2p}: w^2 = 2p(v^4 - u^4)$
contains $(1,1,0)$, and $C'_{-1}: w^2 = -u^4 + 4p^2v^4$ contains $(0,1,2p)$.

**Kill $d = \pm 2$ at $p$.** $C'_2: w^2 = 2(u^4 - p^2v^4)$. Normalize $u, v \in \mathbb{Z}_p$,
$\min(v_p(u), v_p(v)) = 0$.

* If $u \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$: reducing mod $p$, $w^2 \equiv 2u^4$, so $2 \in (\mathbb{F}_p^\times)^2$ — contradicts Lemma A(1).
* If $u = pu_1$ (so $v \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$): $w^2 = 2p^2(p^2u_1^4 - v^4)$, and $p^2u_1^4 - v^4$ is a unit, so $w = pw_1$ with $w_1^2 \equiv -2v^4 \pmod p$, so $-2 \in (\mathbb{F}_p^\times)^2$ — contradicts Lemma A(1).

For $C'_{-2}: w^2 = -2u^4 + 2p^2v^4$ the same two cases give $-2$, resp. $2$, as a square mod $p$ —
both impossible. Hence $C'_{\pm 2}(\mathbb{Q}_p) = \emptyset$.

**Kill $d = \pm p$ at $p$.** $C'_p: w^2 = p(u^4 - 4v^4)$ and $C'_{-p}: w^2 = p(4v^4 - u^4)$.
Normalize as before. If $p$ divides exactly one of $u, v$, then $u^4 - 4v^4$ is a $p$-unit and
$v_p(w^2) = 1$ — impossible; $p$ cannot divide both. So $u, v \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$, and parity of
$v_p(w^2)$ forces $u^4 \equiv 4v^4 \pmod p$, i.e. $4 \in (\mathbb{F}_p^\times)^4$ — contradicting
Lemma A(3). Hence $C'_{\pm p}(\mathbb{Q}_p) = \emptyset$.

Therefore

$$\operatorname{Sel}^{(\varphi)}(E_p/\mathbb{Q}) = \{1, -1, 2p, -2p\} = \operatorname{im}\alpha',
\qquad \Sha(E_p/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi] = 0 . \tag{5.1}$$

## 6. Torsion

**Lemma B.** For every odd prime $p$: $E_p(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}} = \{O, T\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$
and $E'_p(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}} = \{O', T', (\pm 2p, 0)\} \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$.

*Proof.* **2-primary part.** $x^3 + p^2x = x(x^2 + p^2)$ has $x = 0$ as its only rational root, so
$E_p(\mathbb{Q})[2] = \{O, T\}$; $X(X-2p)(X+2p)$ splits, so $E'_p(\mathbb{Q})[2] \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$.
For $y^2 = x^3 + Bx$ the duplication formula reads $x(2P) = \big((x^2 - B)/(2y)\big)^2$. A rational
point of order 4 on $E_p$ would satisfy $2P = T$, i.e. $x^2 = p^2$, i.e. $x = \pm p$ and
$y^2 = \pm 2p^3$: impossible ($v_p(y^2) = 3$ odd, resp. $y^2 < 0$). On $E'_p$ ($B = -4p^2$),
$2P = T'$ needs $X^2 = -4p^2$ (no rational $X$), and $2P = (\pm 2p, 0)$ needs the rational square
$x(2P)$ to equal $\pm 2p$ — but $2p$ is not a square and $-2p < 0$. So neither curve has a rational
point of order 4, and the 2-primary torsion is as claimed.

**Odd part.** Let $\ell$ be an odd prime dividing the torsion order of $E_p(\mathbb{Q})$ (same
argument for $E'_p$). Both curves have good reduction at every prime $q \nmid 2p$
($\Delta(E_p) = -2^6p^6$, $\Delta(E'_p) = 2^{12}p^6$). For $q \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, $q \nmid 2p$:
writing $f(x) = x^3 + Bx$ (an odd polynomial) and $\chi$ for the quadratic character of
$\mathbb{F}_q^\times$,

$$\#E(\mathbb{F}_q) = q + 1 + \sum_{x \in \mathbb{F}_q} \chi(f(x)) = q + 1,$$

because $\chi(f(-x)) = \chi(-1)\chi(f(x)) = -\chi(f(x))$ makes the sum telescope in pairs
$\{x, -x\}$ (and $\chi(f(0)) = 0$). By Dirichlet choose a prime $q \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ with
$q \equiv 1 \pmod \ell$ and $q \nmid 2p$ (the class exists since it is coprime to $4\ell$). Reduction
mod $q$ is injective on prime-to-$q$ torsion (Silverman VII.3.1), so
$\ell \mid \#E(\mathbb{F}_q) = q + 1 \equiv 2 \pmod \ell$, forcing $\ell \mid 2$ — a contradiction. $\square$

## 7. Proof of the Theorem

Let $p \equiv 5 \pmod 8$. By (4.1), (5.1) and the rank identity of Section 2 with
$|E_p(\mathbb{Q})[2]| = 2$ (Lemma B):

$$2^{\,\operatorname{rank} E_p(\mathbb{Q}) + 1} = \tfrac12 \cdot |\operatorname{im}\alpha| \cdot |\operatorname{im}\alpha'|
= \tfrac12 \cdot 1 \cdot 4 = 2, \qquad \text{so } \operatorname{rank} E_p(\mathbb{Q}) = 0 .$$

With Lemma B this gives $E_p(\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ generated by $(0,0)$; the isogenous
curve has the same rank, so $E'_p(\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$ generated by $(0,0)$ and
$(2p, 0)$.

For $\Sha$: if $x \in \Sha(E_p/\mathbb{Q})[2]$, then $\varphi_* x \in \Sha(E'_p/\mathbb{Q})$
satisfies $\hat\varphi_* \varphi_* x = 2x = 0$, so $\varphi_* x \in \Sha(E'_p/\mathbb{Q})[\hat\varphi] = 0$
by (4.1); then $x \in \Sha(E_p/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi] = 0$ by (5.1). Hence
$\Sha(E_p/\mathbb{Q})[2] = 0$. Symmetrically, $x \in \Sha(E'_p/\mathbb{Q})[2]$ implies
$\hat\varphi_* x \in \Sha(E_p/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi] = 0$, so
$x \in \Sha(E'_p/\mathbb{Q})[\hat\varphi] = 0$. A nontrivial 2-primary group contains an element of
order 2, so both $\Sha[2^\infty]$ vanish. $\blacksquare$

## 8. Sharpness of the congruence, and what fails outside it

The congruence $p \equiv 5 \pmod 8$ entered only through Lemma A, and the method detects its own
boundary:

* **$p \equiv 1 \pmod 8$:** $-1$ **is** a fourth power mod $p$ (take $z$ of order 8), so the
  obstruction of Section 4 at the place $p$ for $C_p$ disappears and
  $\operatorname{Sel}^{(\hat\varphi)}$ can grow; the present proof gives no rank-0 conclusion.
* **$p \equiv 3 \pmod 8$:** $(-2/p) = +1$, so the mod-$p$ kill of $C'_{-2}$ fails.
* **$p \equiv 7 \pmod 8$:** $(2/p) = +1$, so the mod-$p$ kill of $C'_2$ fails.

In the last two cases $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, and the standard twisting formula for root numbers
predicts $W(E_p) = -1$, i.e. odd analytic rank, so no rank-0 statement should be expected there;
we do **not** prove any root-number claim in this note and use none.

## 9. Honesty

**Honesty tag (one line).** *The statement is very likely classical or folklore for experts in
quartic twists of $j=1728$ curves (the machinery is Fermat descent as in Silverman X.4.9); the
complete, self-contained proof above — candidate lists via the support lemma, all seven kills on
one side and four on the other at named places, exact Selmer groups, Mordell–Weil groups, and
$\Sha[2] = 0$ — was worked out from scratch here, and no priority is claimed.*

Itemized:

1. **What is original here vs. cited.** The descent formalism (the maps $\alpha, \alpha'$, the
   homogeneous-space criterion, the exact sequences) is classical and cited to Silverman X.4.2,
   X.4.9. Everything family-specific is proved in full: the support lemma (Step 0), the rank
   identity in the exact form used (including the verification $T' \notin \varphi(E(\mathbb{Q}))$),
   Lemma A, all eleven local kills, Lemma B, and the $\Sha$ argument.
2. **Disjointness from the Wave-2 family.** Wave 2 treated $y^2 = x^3 - q^2x$, $q \equiv 3 \pmod 8$.
   Quartic twists $y^2 = x^3 + Ax$ and $y^2 = x^3 + A'x$ are quadratic twists of one another iff
   $A'/A$ is a rational square. Here $A'/A = -q^2/p^2 < 0$ is not a square, so no curve of the
   present family is a quadratic twist (in particular, not isomorphic or 2-isogenous over
   $\mathbb{Q}$ in the twist class) of any Wave-2 curve, even though both families have $j = 1728$.
   The congruence classes ($5 \bmod 8$ vs. $3 \bmod 8$) and the shape of the Selmer computation
   (here the surviving classes $\pm 1, \pm 2p$ are forced by the full rational 2-torsion of $E'_p$)
   also differ.
3. **What is *not* proved.** No claim about $L(E_p, 1)$, BSD, or the full finiteness of $\Sha$;
   we prove only $\Sha[2^\infty] = 0$ for both curves. The root-number remark in Section 8 is an
   expectation, flagged as such, and is not used anywhere. Nothing is claimed for
   $p \not\equiv 5 \pmod 8$.
4. **No unchecked local conditions.** The four surviving classes of
   $\operatorname{Sel}^{(\varphi)}$ contain global points, so their everywhere-local solubility is
   automatic; each killed class was killed at a single explicitly named place ($\infty$, $2$, or
   $p$), which suffices. The kills at $\infty$ and $2$ hold for all odd $p$; only the kills at $p$
   use $p \equiv 5 \pmod 8$.
5. **Numerical sanity check (not part of the proof).** PARI/GP (`ellrank`, `elltors`) confirms
   rank $0$, $E_p(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ and
   $E'_p(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}} \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$ for all eight primes
   $p \equiv 5 \pmod 8$ with $p < 110$.
